
This INMO problem <span style="color: var(--lColorAc);">($\textit{1992, Q.1.}$) [1]</span> calls for an angle bisector to get similar triangles. The problem says:

<div style="border: 1px solid var(--lColorAc); padding: 12px; border-radius: 4px; margin-top: 12px;">

In a $\triangle ABC$, $\angle A = 2 \angle B$. Prove that $a^2 = b(b + c)$.

</div>
<br>
Below is a rough sketch based on given information.

<br>

<div style="text-align: center;">
<img src="./posts/0002/0002_1992_inmo_q1.svg" alt="" style="width: 85%; height: auto;" />
</div>

<br>

Draw angle bisector $AD$ of $\angle A$.

<br>

Let angles, $\angle ABC = x^\circ$. $\Rightarrow \angle BAC = 2x^\circ$ (given). And both, $\angle BAD = \angle DAC =x^\circ$ (by construction).

<br>

Let sides, $AB=c$, $BC=a$, $CA=b$ and $BD=d$. $\Rightarrow CD =a-d$.

<br>

$\because \angle DBA = \angle BAD = x^\circ$, $\Rightarrow \triangle BAD$ is isoceles triangle with $BD=DA=d$.

<br>

In $\triangle ABC$, $\angle ACB = 180^\circ-3x^\circ$ (by interior angle sum property of a triangle).

<br>

Examine, $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle DAC$:

<br>

$\angle ABC = \angle DAC = x^\circ$

<br>

$\angle ACB = \angle DCA = 180^\circ-3x^\circ$ (common angle).

<br>

Hence, $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DAC$ such that;

<br>

$\dfrac{AB}{DA}=\dfrac{BC}{AC}=\dfrac{CA}{CD}$

<br>

$= \dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{b}{a-d}$

<br>

$\Rightarrow d=\dfrac{bc}{a}$

<br>

Also, 

<br>

$a(a-d)=b^2$

<br>

$\Rightarrow a(a-\dfrac{bc}{a})=b^2$

<br>

$\Rightarrow a^2-bc=b^2$

<br>

$\Rightarrow a^2=b^2+bc$

<br>

resulting,

<br>

<div style="border: 1px solid var(--lColorAc); padding: 12px; border-radius: 4px; margin-top: 12px; text-align: center;">
$a^2 = b(b + c)$
</div>

<br>

<div style="text-align: justify; width: 90%;margin: 0 auto;">
We see how a simple construction of angle bisector helped us solving this problem. The motivation to draw angle bisector was in given information where one angle of the triangle was given twice of the other. Drawing angle bisector made equal base angles that helped in making an isoceles triangle. And later established ratios using similar triangles that solved the problem.
</div>
