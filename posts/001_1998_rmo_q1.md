
We will discuss an old RMO problem <span style="color: var(--lColorAc);">($\textit{1998, Q.1.}$)</span> in this post. The problem says:

<div style="border: 1px solid var(--lColorAc); padding: 12px; border-radius: 4px; margin-top: 12px;">

Let $ABCD$ be a convex quadrilateral in which $\angle BAC = 50^{\circ}, \angle CAD = 60^{\circ}$, $\angle CBD = 30^{\circ}$ and $\angle BDC = 25^{\circ}$. If $E$ is the point of intersection of $AC$ and $BD$, find $\angle AEB$.

</div>
<br>
Let us first draw a rough sketch with given information.

<br>

<div style="text-align: center;">
<img src="./posts/001_1998_rmo_q1_01.svg" alt="" style="width: 85%; height: auto;" />
</div>

<br>

It is interesting to observe that $\angle CAB = 2 \times \angle BDC$, and both the angles are made on segment $BC$. 
Similarly, $\angle CAD = 2 \times \angle DBC$, and both the angles are made on segment $CD$.

This clearly suggests a vital insight to solve this problem i.e. $A$ is center of circumcircle of $\triangle BCD$. And the $AD, AC$ and $AB$ are the radius of the same circle.
<br>
$\Rightarrow AD=AC=AB$. <br> $\therefore \angle ACD = 60^\circ$ and $\angle ADE = 35^\circ$, implying:
<br>

<div style="border: 1px solid var(--lColorAc); padding: 12px; border-radius: 4px; margin-top: 12px; text-align: center;">

$\angle AEB=95^\circ$.

</div>

<br>
Now when the problem is solved, the question still remains.
<br><br>

</div>
